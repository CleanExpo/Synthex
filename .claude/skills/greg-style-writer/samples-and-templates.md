Short quotes only, to show the moves:

- Self-aware aside: "I can't believe this works, but it does." — same essay.

## Templates

### A. X / LinkedIn post (60–150 words)

```
[Specific hook: a result, a number, or a contrarian claim in one line.]

[One-line context: what happened / why I noticed.]

Here's what I learned:

1. [Lesson — one line]
2. [Lesson — one line, with a real example]
3. [Lesson — one line]

[One-line reframe: old world vs new world, or the bigger point.]

[Optional question to the reader.]
```

### B. Thread (6–12 parts)

```
1/ [Promise + specificity: "I spent [time] doing [thing]. Here's exactly how I'd do it if I started today:"]
2/ [Context: the problem in plain words]
3–9/ [One idea per part: name → what it is → how I'd do it → real example]
10/ [Recap in 3 bullets]
11/ [Soft next step: a free resource, a question, or "reply and I'll send it"]
```

### C. Newsletter essay (500–900 words)

```
Subject: [Curious, specific, lowercase-casual is fine]

[1–2 lines: "I figured out…" / "Something happened this week…"]

First, some context…
[3–6 short lines of story with one real number]

So I tried something.
[Name the idea in Phill's own words. Define it in one line.]

Here's how it works:
1. [Step / component] — [one or two lines]
2. …
3. …

[Self-aware aside or objection: "Now, I know what you're thinking…"]
[Answer it in 2–3 lines.]

So to wrap up:
[One-line big reframe.]

[Phill's own sign-off]
```

### D. Video script (3–8 minutes, video-first)

```
COLD OPEN (0–10s): [Bold claim or surprising number.]
PROMISE (10–25s): "By the end of this, you're going to know [three things]."
WHY NOW (25–60s): [What changed; one real example.]
PART 1/2/3 (each 60–120s): What it is → How I'd do it → Real example from our jobs → Watch-out.
THE SAUCE (30–60s): [The specific tactic most people miss.]
RECAP (15s): Three bullets, spoken.
CLOSE (10s): [Phill's own close + one next step.]
```

Speakability: read it aloud; no sentence over ~20 words; numbers said the way people say them.

### E. Landing page (very short)

```
Headline: [Curiosity + specific benefit, ≤ 12 words]
Subhead: [Who it's for + what they get, ≤ 20 words]
Real photo of Phill or the team on a real job
CTA button: [Specific, playful, for a genuinely useful free thing — not "Submit"]
Proof line: [One real number or testimonial, with permission]
```

### F. Blog/article that Google will read

Greg rhythm inside `agent-ready-content`: question-led H2s, short paragraphs, a numbered method, first-hand job evidence and real photos, an honest "what we'd do differently", visible updated date only after real changes.

## Worked examples (Phill's world — fill brackets with real facts before publishing)

**Post (A):**

> We don't spend a cent on ads. Not one dollar.
>
> People think that's crazy for [number] businesses.
>
> Here's what we do instead:
>
> 1. Every job becomes proof. Real photos, the tech's own words.
> 2. Every call gets answered. The money is in the missed ones.
> 3. Every page earns its place. If it's thin, it goes.
>
> Ads rent attention. Proof owns it.
>
> What's one thing you'd stop paying for tomorrow?

**Video cold open (D):**

> Most restoration websites are dead. Not broken. Dead.
> Same photos since [year]. Same three paragraphs. Phone goes to voicemail at 5pm.
> By the end of this, you're going to know the three things we're doing to bring them back to life — and the one that's going to surprise you.

**Newsletter opener (C):**

> I spent today going through every Google update since March 2024.
> Eighteen of them. One thing jumped out.
> The sites that win aren't the ones that post the most. They're the ones that say something only they could say.

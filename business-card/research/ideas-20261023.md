## Human moments

Source: `~/2nd Brain/2nd Brain/Wiki/living-card/lc-08-human-moments.md` (23/10/2026). Ranked by memorability x ease. Not legal advice.

1. **Remove the draw: everyone who finishes the hunt gets the reward.** Replace "you are in the draw" (card.html line 341) with a completable 5-rabbit hunt whose reward everyone gets (full playbook, template pack or a short personal video from Phill). No chance element means no trade promotion lottery, so no lottery T&Cs, permits or draw records. It is still a promotional offer: state the condition and reward plainly and deliver it (ACL s 32). Evidence: VIC, QLD and NSW regulator pages; IKEA effect needs completion. Ease: very high (copy change plus unlock screen).
2. **Rabbit 1 is already on the card: "You've found 1 of 5. The other 4 are in the talk."** Endowed-progress head start, with a reason given. Unfinished hunts bring people back (Ovsiankina effect), so the card gets reopened. Do not claim it makes people remember you better: the Zeigarnik memory effect failed a 2025 meta-analysis. Ease: high.
3. **"What's the one thing you'll do on Monday?"** At the end of the talk the person types one line into the card in their own words. With recorded consent, the follow-up quotes their own sentence back. The sentence plus a name is personal information: store it with the consent record, cover it in the privacy notice, delete on request (see lc-04/lc-05). Generation and self-reference effects (secondary sources). Ease: high (one text field, stored with the consent record).
4. **Design the ending, not just the opener (peak-end).** After "Save to my phone", the last screen is a deliberate peak: their name, the sound, and "here's what I'll send you and when". At the talk, the final minute is the last rabbit reveal, not a thank-you slide. Kahneman 1993: 69% chose to repeat the longer, better-ending trial. Ease: high.
5. **Room counter on the final slide: "187 of you found all five."** Live count of completed hunts shown on stage. A collective, visible moment with no chance and no prize, so no lottery rules. Gives people something to show the person next to them. Ease: medium (needs a small live counter endpoint).
6. **Curiosity gap: ask in the talk, answer on the card.** Open one half-known question early in the talk ("one reading tells you whether this job ends up in court: which one?") and put the answer behind the final rabbit. Loewenstein (secondary). Ease: high (content, not code).
7. **The 2am save (product feature for restorers buying the card).** On "Save to my phone", offer a contact label people search in a panic ("Water damage – emergency") plus the lock-screen QR. The card's job for a restoration buyer is to be findable at the moment of need. Inference from STEPPS triggers; no restoration-specific study found. Ease: medium (vCard label/notes field).
8. **Make the find visible and worth showing.** Keep the sound and confetti audible and visible to neighbours. The finder gets a "you know where rabbit 3 is" moment, and the reveal is an awe beat, not a coupon. Never place the rabbit gift next to the review step (Google bans review incentives; lc-02). Berger & Milkman 2012: high-arousal awe content is shared more. Ease: high (mostly exists; tune the reveal).

## Gaps

Source: `~/2nd Brain/2nd Brain/Wiki/living-card/lc-07-gaps.md` (23/10/2026). Ranked by impact on getting the owner more customers. Tags as on that page.

1. **The contact may not land on Android.** A browser can't hand a .vcf straight to Contacts on Android ("no solution for Android yet") [VERIFIED: outsystems.com forum 97467]. Our hosted save just navigates to the .vcf [INFERENCE: card.html ~1006]. Fix: one-line platform-specific next step after the tap, visible Copy number / Copy email fallback, try Web Share with a .vcf file on Android [UNCONFIRMED: needs device test], and count a save only when the recipient confirms.
2. **Follow-up never starts.** Exhibitor handout: "87% of tradeshow leads ... never effectively followed up on" [VERIFIED: NAA PDF; underlying study not reached, treat as lore]. Fix: each confirmed save or opened personalised link creates a "met" record (name, event, date) and a same-day draft note for the owner to approve, sent only to people who shared details and agreed; owner screen shows "met / followed up".
3. **No way to share back, with consent.** Blinq's lead form replaces the card and its setup guide doesn't mention consent [VERIFIED: support.blinq.me 76263]; APP 3.6: collect "only from the individual" [VERIFIED: OAIC APP 3]. Fix: optional "Swap details" after the save (name, mobile or email, "what do you need?"), one-line purpose, separate unticked news box, consent text and timestamp stored, no third-party enrichment.
4. **A week later they've forgotten who you were.** Blinq sells "where you met" and a photo [VERIFIED: blinq.me/solutions/enrichment]; our vCard has no PHOTO and a fixed NOTE [INFERENCE: card.html 511–523]. Fix: NOTE = "Met at [event], DD/MM/YYYY. [what Phill does for you]"; add a small base64 PHOTO but don't rely on it (iOS import unreliable) [VERIFIED: developer.apple.com thread 822738].
5. **No signal on the floor means no card for the recipient.** "unreliable connectivity as the default condition" [VERIFIED: integrate.com ?p=4488]; our QRs encode a URL [INFERENCE: card.html 892–894]. Fix: a second "No signal? Scan this one" QR that encodes the vCard itself (small, no photo) on the owner screen and lock-screen image.
6. **Billing traps kill trust in the card product.** Mobilo: 11 of 12 negative reviews on the page fetched are renewal or surprise charges [VERIFIED: trustpilot mobilocard.com]; subscription traps banned from 01/07/2027 [VERIFIED: ia.acs.org.au JustAnswer article]. Fix: one-tap cancel, priced renewal reminder, plain refund rule, and say so on the sales page.
7. **People are wary of scanning QR codes.** QR scams impersonate myGov and the ATO [UNCONFIRMED: CHOICE, snippet]. Fix: print the short domain under every QR, first screen shows the owner's face and name, never ask for login or payment after a scan.
8. **Unasked-for sound.** Code says the score starts "on the visitor's first tap anywhere" [INFERENCE: card.html 617–620]; WCAG 1.4.2 covers audio over 3 seconds [VERIFIED: w3.org Understanding audio-control]. A mute button exists. Fix: start music only from the sound button itself, so a stranger tapping Save in a quiet meeting gets silence.

## Tech wow

Source: `~/2nd Brain/2nd Brain/Wiki/living-card/lc-06-tech-wow.md` (23/10/2026). Ranked by wow x feasibility. Feasibility means it works in iOS Safari and Android Chrome today, with no paid account and no new app. Ruled out: App Clips (needs a full native app and the Apple program), Android Instant Apps (shut down from December 2025), Chrome built-in AI (desktop only), WebXR (no iOS), Web Share Target (no iOS), NFC Wallet passes (Apple entitlement). Not legal advice.

1. **Real-scan Phill in your room (AR).** One `<model-viewer>` tag opens AR Quick Look on iPhone (USDZ, iOS 12+) and Scene Viewer on Android (glb, ARCore phones). The recipient stands a life-size real scan of Phill, or the real drying kit, in their office, with a banner via Quick Look's `#custom=` or `#callToAction=` parameters (not yet confirmed that a banner tap can trigger the contact save). Real photogrammetry only; a generated avatar breaks `.claude/rules/real-images-only.md`. AR cards exist (ARLOOPA), but a life-size real person with a banner was not found. Ease: high once a scan exists. Free (Apache 2.0).
2. **Living glass parallax.** The glass layers and light shift as the phone tilts (DeviceMotion/Orientation). iOS 14.5+ needs `requestPermission()` from a tap, so ask on the existing first tap. Must still look right if motion is denied or reduced motion is on. Ease: high.
3. **Live presence glow.** "4 people at today's expo are holding this card right now", via Supabase Realtime Presence. Show counts and anonymous dots only. Never put names or `?to=` values in the shared channel. Ease: high.
4. **Spatial sound that follows the tilt.** HRTF `PannerNode` (iOS 6+, Android) on the sound the card already makes, positioned by tilt. No microphone, no permission. Ease: high.
5. **Haptic crack.** A physical thud when the glass breaks. `navigator.vibrate` works on Android after a tap; iOS has no Vibration API, and the hidden switch-control workaround is best effort and may already be patched. Ease: high on Android, fragile on iOS.
6. **Ask the card.** A typed or spoken question, answered aloud by a device voice labelled as "the card", never as Phill. `speechSynthesis` works on both phones; `SpeechRecognition` needs the `webkit` prefix on iOS and sends audio to a server unless `processLocally` is set. Default to typing, make the microphone optional, store no audio. Ease: medium (server AI cost, privacy notice).
7. **Photo QR art.** The owner QR becomes a halftone of Phill's real photo at a high error-correction level, tested with two phone cameras. Use deterministic halftoning of owned photos, or the sanctioned `image-generation.ts` path. Do not use free-standing generative QR models. Ease: high.
8. **Show-date Wallet pass.** Builds on lc-01: an Apple pass with `relevantDates` (`relevantDate` is deprecated) and `locations`, so it surfaces on the lock screen at the next show. After the event, a back-field update says what you discussed. Promotional updates need Spam Act consent. Costs the Apple program ("$99" as shown on the page; currency not stated) plus Google Wallet issuer onboarding. Ease: medium.

# Competitor teardown: Advanced SE versus CCW

Observed 08/11/2026. Every row below was read on the live page on that date by the lead session,
unless it is tagged `[UNCONFIRMED]` (found by a research helper and not reopened). Nothing from
Advanced SE is reused in CCW's videos: no wording, footage, product photos or logo. Their material
is used only to decide what CCW should say differently.

## The short version

- CCW's real web shop is **ccwonline.com.au**. `ccwarehouse.com.au` is used for email
  (sales@ccwarehouse.com.au) and refused a direct connection on 08/11/2026. Every campaign link goes
  to ccwonline.com.au.
- Advanced SE (ASE) sells on fitouts, training, hire and a start-up bundle, and says "best prices,
  guaranteed".
- CCW sells on its own Razorback range, made in house; parts depth with phone diagnosis and
  same-day dispatch; three company-run sites (QLD, NSW, VIC); prices shown openly online; and
  buying advice written by people who do the work.
- Both are publishing again: CCW last posted 22/10/2026, ASE posted twice on 16/10/2026. Neither
  runs a storm-season series or short how-to videos. That is the opening for this campaign.

## Gaps CCW can own

1. **Storm season, told by the people who stock the drying gear.** The 2026/27 cyclone season runs
   01/11 to 30/04; modelling points to fewer cyclones than usual (ABC, 01/11/2026), but storms, flash
   flooding and burst pipes still fill restorers' diaries. Neither supplier runs a storm-readiness
   series. CCW stocks the drying range (XPOWER, Dri-Eaz Velo, Razorback MaxxGuard, Injectidry, Water
   Claw) and can teach how to be ready before the calls start.
2. **Short, practical videos.** CCW's shop pages already hold expert, plain advice ("four correctly
   placed units will beat two powerful ones"). Turning that advice into 30-second videos is a format
   ASE does not run.
3. **Its own range, backed locally.** Razorback is CCW's own, made in house, with spares held in
   Australia. ASE has no named own range to answer with.
4. **Parts and phone diagnosis.** "Most faults diagnosed over the phone, part dispatched the same day"
   is CCW's strongest service promise. The "service your equipment" week is built on it.
5. **Prices in the open.** CCW shows prices, truckmounts included. Videos can say "the price is on
   the page" without naming a competitor.
6. **Three company-run sites.** QLD, NSW and VIC, plus agents in SA, WA and TAS.
7. **Toby as the expert voice.** His own guides (UV pet urine, viscose rugs) become "what you may not
   know" topics. Margot presents; Toby's guide is the cited source.

Where CCW should not compete in this campaign: training (ASE's flood house), equipment hire and
start-up bundles. Starting hire or service plans is a business decision for Toby, not a video.
Fixable basics for CCW's own site: show the free-delivery rule (if any) and the GST basis next to
prices; neither site does this.

## Rules for the videos that follow from this

- Never name, show or imply Advanced SE. Comparative claims are out; CCW's own strengths are in.
- Every CCW claim in a script comes from a CCW page listed below, and repeats CCW's own words (for
  example "diagnose most problems over the phone" stays CCW's claim, not a figure we add to).
- Prices are not put in videos: they change, and the GST basis is not shown. Videos send people to
  the product page.

# Why, five layers deep

Each layer answers "why?" about the layer before it. Every claim points to a row in
`standards-register.md` (R numbers). A claim marked UNVERIFIED there is used here only as a
question to answer, never as a fact. The definition at the end is built from the five layers,
not asserted.

Starting question, from the six reels: why does one contractor's mould job stay fixed while
another's comes back in six months?

## Layer 1: What the viewer sees

The professional job looks different, and the six reels show how:

- The area is sealed off before anything is cut (reel 2, reel 5). (R1)
- Air is drawn out through HEPA filtration so the sealed area stays under negative pressure (reel 2). (R2)
- Dust is caught at the point of cutting, not swept up later (reel 3). (R8, R9)
- Damaged porous material comes out; it is not painted over (reel 2, reel 4). (R3)
- Workers wear fitted respirators, overalls and gloves (reel 4). (R12)
- Someone names the cause, not just the mould (reel 1), and someone checks the result (reel 2's last step). (R15, R6)

Why do those visible steps matter? Layer 2.

## Layer 2: Why each step works (the physics and biology)

- Mould needs water. Remove the mould but leave the water, and growth starts again. The EPA and SafeWork NSW both say so. (R15)
- Spores ride on dust. Cutting or scraping throws spores into the air, and they settle anywhere the air goes. Sealing and negative pressure make the air go one way: into the filter. (R1, R2, R8)
- Porous materials hold growth inside them, where wiping cannot reach. So plasterboard, insulation and carpet that grew mould are removed, not cleaned. (R3)
- Dead mould still causes reactions, so killing it is not enough; it has to be physically removed. (R24)
- Wet materials left more than 24 to 48 hours become the next mould job. (R29)
- Some moulds tell time: a slow grower such as Chaetomium suggests the material stayed wet for a long time, which points back to the cause. (R13, R14: unverified, so used as a question for the lab, not a claim)

Why should a business be held to these steps rather than doing what is fastest? Layer 3.

## Layer 3: Why a standard and the law require it

- Australia now has its own standards: AS-IICRC S500:2025 for water damage and AS-IICRC S520:2025 for mould. They turn the physics into a written procedure that insurers and scopes can call up. (R21)
- Work health and safety law puts duties on the business: fitted P2 respirators, training, hand washing, and controlling dust. (R12, R22, R31)
- The same law makes the other hazards non-optional. Before a wall in an older home is cut: asbestos (pre-1990), lead paint (pre-1970), live wiring, sewage-contaminated water. (R18, R19, R23, R27)
- There are no safe-air numbers for mould, so the job cannot be "passed" by one air test. It is judged against a procedure and a visual and documented verification. (R28, R6)
- The standard itself draws a line: correcting the cause belongs to the owner or their agent unless the remediator is contracted to do it. A professional says so in writing, so the cause does not fall between two parties. (R26)

Why does following a written standard make someone a professional and not just compliant? Layer 4.

## Layer 4: What makes the provider professional

Compliance is the floor. A professional provider is the one who can prove, after they leave, what they did and why:

- Trained and certified people: IICRC WRT for water; AMRT for mould (which needs WRT first). Named on the job, not just in the logo. (R20)
- The right equipment, of a known standard: HEPA filtration, HEPA vacuums, dehumidifiers and air movers, moisture meters, PPE that fits. (R2, R4, R12)
- A written plan before work starts, revised when hidden damage turns up (reel 4's lesson). (R1, R15)
- Documentation: moisture readings, photos at each stage, waste bagged and recorded, the hazard checks done. (R18, R30)
- Independent verification at the end rather than marking their own homework. (R6)
- Honesty about limits: no "kills mould", no made-up numbers, no implied licence. (R5, R7, R20, R24)

Why does that matter to the person paying? Layer 5.

## Layer 5: Why it matters to the homeowner, the insurer and the business

- The homeowner: the mould does not come back, the family is not exposed during the work, and the house is not contaminated further. That is the actual product. (R15, R1, R2)
- The insurer or property manager: a documented, standard-referenced job closes the claim once. An undocumented one reopens it. (R21)
- The worker: they go home healthy. (R12, R18, R31)
- The business: work that can be proven is work that can be defended, reviewed and sold again. Every video we make is a public promise of that proof.

## What is a Professional Restoration Provider

Built from the five layers above:

A Professional Restoration Provider is a business whose trained and certified people find and
name the cause of the water or mould problem, control the work so contamination and hazards do
not spread to the occupants or the workers, remove what cannot be cleaned, follow the current
Australian standards (AS-IICRC S500:2025 for water, AS-IICRC S520:2025 for mould) and work health
and safety law, document every stage, and have the result verified independently, so the owner,
the insurer and the next tradesperson can all see the job was done right and will stay done.

Five tests a viewer can apply to any provider, each traced to a layer:

1. Did they tell you the cause, in writing? (Layer 2, Layer 3)
2. Did they seal and filter the work area before cutting? (Layer 1, Layer 2)
3. Did they check for asbestos, lead and wiring in an older home first? (Layer 3)
4. Can they show you their certifications and your job's photos and readings? (Layer 4)
5. Who checks their work at the end, and is it someone else? (Layer 4, Layer 5)

These five tests are the spine of every restoration video script we write: each video should
make at least one of them visible on screen.

## LI-02 — Listicle: 5 things every professional should know

**5 things every restoration and facilities professional should know about H5 bird flu**

1. **Reporting comes before cleaning.** If you find sick or dead birds or animals, do not touch them, record what you see, and report them to the Emergency Animal Disease Hotline on 1800 675 888.

2. **Clean first, disinfect second, document third.** Cleaning before disinfection is a core control principle — disinfectants do their job on a cleaned surface, not a soiled one.

3. **You work under authority direction, not instead of it.** Restoration and IAQ members can assist with risk assessment, documentation, containment planning, cleaning records, disinfection records and client communication — under the direction of the responding authorities.

4. **Worker safety is the job, not an add-on.** The course covers WHS, PPE, fit checking, hygiene and exposure response before any site task.

5. **Know your boundaries.** Professionals support government, veterinary, wildlife and public-health authorities — they never replace them. Any disinfection method, including dry fogging, is label-led and SDS-led, never a substitute for official biosecurity response.

Everything above is exactly what the new CARSI course teaches:

## LI-04 — Clean first, disinfect second, document third

One sentence carries most of the discipline in this work: clean first, disinfect second, document third.

Cleaning before disinfection is a core control principle. Source removal and detergent cleaning come before any disinfectant, because a disinfectant works on a cleaned surface, not on organic load. The documentation then records what was actually done — product, dilution, contact time, operator and PPE.

The CARSI course walks through this sequence the way a job runs, not the way a brochure reads.

https://carsi.com.au/courses/avian-influenza-awareness-restoration-iaq-facilities

## LI-05 — Worker safety and PPE

Before any surface is touched, the question is whether the worker is protected.

The CARSI H5 readiness course puts worker safety first: WHS risk assessment, PPE selection, fit checking, hygiene and exposure response. Required PPE follows the site's hazard assessment — it is not guesswork and it is stated before work begins.

Safe work is not the reward for a good job. It is the condition for starting one.

## LI-06 — Professional boundaries

The most professional thing a restoration business can say on an H5 site is where its role ends.

Restoration and IAQ members can assist with risk assessment, documentation, containment planning, cleaning records, disinfection records and client communication — under authority direction. They do not replace government, veterinary, wildlife or public-health authorities, and they should never be marketed as if they do.

The course teaches those boundaries as clearly as it teaches the tasks.

## LI-07 — Documentation that stands up

If it is not documented, it did not happen — at least not for the client, the insurer or the authority.

CARSI's readiness pathway pairs with RestoreAssist field documentation: authority contacted and time, observations without handling, exclusion-zone photos, worker roster, PPE and fit-check notes, clean and dirty zone plans, cleaning and disinfection logs, and any product, batch, dilution and contact time. Exceptions and limitations are recorded too.

Good records are how a professional site work becomes a defensible record.

https://carsi.com.au/avian-influenza-readiness

# Blog (1) — launch article for the hub

## BL-01 — H5 bird flu readiness for restoration, cleaning and IAQ professionals: the new CARSI course

**Standfirst:** A dead bird is not a cleanup job first. It is a reporting job first. CARSI's new course teaches restoration, cleaning, IAQ and facility teams how to respond calmly, safely and within their professional boundaries — using official Australian Government advice.

### Why this course, and why now

Australia is seeing H5 bird flu detections in wild birds, and professionals who work across sites, buildings and facilities are asking a reasonable question: what is my role if I find a sick or dead bird?

The answer is calmer than the headlines. As of the DAFF update of 5 July 2026, DAFF reported six confirmed H5 detections in wild birds — four in Western Australia, one in South Australia and one in New South Wales. DAFF states there is no evidence of mass mortality and no evidence of infection in poultry or the wider agriculture industry, and the Australian Centre for Disease Control states the current risk to people in Australia is low. Please check current DAFF advice for the latest status.

CARSI's new course, **Australian H5 bird flu awareness for restoration, cleaning, IAQ and facility teams**, exists to turn that calm, official position into practical readiness — before it is ever needed.

### The one thing to remember

If you find sick or dead birds or animals:

- Do not touch them.
- Avoid contact.
- Record what you see.
- Report them to the 24-hour Emergency Animal Disease Hotline on **1800 675 888**.

Reporting comes before cleaning. Everything else in the course builds on that.

### What the course teaches

The course is the first module of CARSI's 7-module H5 readiness pathway. It works through:

1. **Awareness and reporting** — the pathway above, and how to record an observation without handling anything.
2. **Worker safety** — WHS risk assessment, PPE, fit checking, hygiene and exposure response. Required PPE follows the site's hazard assessment; it is decided before work begins.
3. **Clean and dirty zones** — exclusion zones, access control and safe transitions.
4. **Clean first, disinfect second, document third** — cleaning before disinfection is a core control principle. Source removal and detergent cleaning come before any disinfectant.
5. **Field documentation** — capturing what was actually done, so the record stands up for the client, the insurer and the authority.
6. **Professional boundaries** — restoration and IAQ members assist under authority direction; they do not replace government, veterinary, wildlife or public-health authorities.

Any disinfection method, including dry fogging, is treated the same way: label-led, SDS-led and legally compliant, and never presented as a substitute for official biosecurity response or as a product that "kills" the virus.

### Who should take it

- Restoration technicians and supervisors.
- Commercial and specialised cleaning teams.
- Indoor air quality (IAQ) professionals.
- Facility, strata and property managers who oversee sites and contractors.

It is self-paced and Australian-produced, so a whole team can share the same baseline on their own schedule.
